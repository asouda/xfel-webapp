Δρ(r) ∝ Σ (|FLIGHT| − |FDARK|) exp(iφref)

This is the difference-Fourier map between two isomorphous datasets. Positive density means more electrons in the light structure. Negative density means more electrons in the dark structure. A real shift is a negative peak on the old position next to a positive peak on the new one.

How it is calculated

  1. Both structure-factor mmCIF files, and both validation coefficient files, are converted to MTZ with cif2mtz.
  2. refmac5 runs for zero cycles so the amplitudes sit in a consistent MTZ. The deposited atoms are not moved.
  3. cad puts light amplitudes, dark amplitudes, and one phase column in a single file. The default phases are PHWT from the light validation 2Fo−Fc coefficients. You can switch to the dark validation file, or to phases from either zero-cycle REFMAC job.
  4. scaleit scales the light amplitudes onto the dark reference. fft transforms |FLIGHT| − |FDARK| with those phases, and extends pads the map around the light model.
  5. Headless Coot clips a sphere around the site. PyMOL draws nested ±nσ isosurfaces, green through blue for positive density and red through magenta for negative density, and writes a PNG plus a .pml script.
{% elif spec.slug == 'refinement' %}

Δρ(r) ∝ Σ (m|Fo| − D|Fc|) exp(iφc)

REFMAC stores that coefficient as DELFWT / PHDELWT. Positive density is present in the observations and missing from the omit model. Negative density is where the model places electrons the data do not support. No second dataset is scaled in.

How it is calculated

  1. The observed mmCIF is converted to MTZ. ctruncate turns intensities I, SIGI into amplitudes, and cad copies the FREE flag onto those amplitudes.
  2. refmac5 refines the omit coordinates for the number of cycles you set. Zero cycles calculate the map without moving atoms. Loose site distances can be external restraints, or a widened libcheck monomer library. Site angles are always external restraints, in degrees.
  3. fft transforms DELFWT / PHDELWT. extends pads the map around the refined model.
  4. Coot clips the site from that difference map. PyMOL draws the refined coordinates and the ±nσ surfaces, and saves the PNG and the .pml script beside the map.
{% else %}

ρ(r) ∝ Σ FWT exp(−ΔB (sinθ/λ)2) exp(iφWT)

This is not a difference map. FWT is already the weighted 2m|Fo| − D|Fc| amplitude from the wwPDB validation file, and PHWT is the model phase. Mesh that covers an atom is accounted-for density. A gap on an atom, or a blob with no atom, is the usual hint that the model and the data disagree. Levels of 1–2σ are the normal view; 3–5σ is for difference maps and will often leave this mesh empty.

How it is calculated

  1. The structure-factor mmCIF and the validation coefficient mmCIF are converted to MTZ.
  2. fft sharpens FWT with SCALE F1 1 ΔB and transforms it with PHWT. Negative ΔB boosts the high-resolution terms. The default −40 Å2 is a sharpening of 40 Å2. ΔB = 0 leaves FWT unchanged.
  3. extends pads the map around the model. Coot exports a normal map fragment, not a difference map.
  4. PyMOL superimposes the site and neighbouring residues and draws one isosurface at each requested σ. The scene script is saved next to the extended map and the PNG.
